# Shortlist — 15 Track A, 15 Track B

Drawn from 181 verified programmes. Ranked on fit to the actual profile — Tunisian
national, 180-ECTS Licence in Business Intelligence from a **private** institution,
French and English fluent, Python/SQL/ML/Power BI/Talend, videography and music
production as practice, no media degree — not on prestige.

**Cost column** is tuition only, per year, for a non-EU applicant, as published.
Living costs are excluded because only 11% of rows carry an official figure and the
brief forbids estimates. "After funding" shows the realistic figure if the named
scheme lands; where nothing plausible exists, it says so.

**Four filters did the real work**, in this order:

1. **Prior-degree subject.** Most Track B masters require a media or fine-art bachelor.
   That is an eligibility rule, not a preference, and it eliminates more good matches
   than cost does.
2. **The two-year question.** The Tunisian equivalence *arrêté* (art. 8/9) wants two
   years of study after the licence. Article 5 lets commissions waive that where ECTS
   are sufficient, so a one-year master is **discretionary risk, not disqualification** —
   but a 120-ECTS two-year programme carries no such risk at all. Marked **2yr** below.
3. **Specific prior credits.** Corvinus wants 18–24 ECTS in named categories; Vilnius
   20 ECTS in economics; EMAI 12 ECTS each of maths, programming and computing science.
   Audit the transcript before applying.
4. **Cost after realistic funding**, not sticker price.

## What this list is not

It is not ranked by employability, salary or reputation — none of those were in the
brief and none were verified, so including them would be invention.

It excludes everything the level filter caught: MBAs, *máster propio*, *Mastère
Spécialisé*, Italian *Master di primo livello*, continuing-education programmes, and
the four Cypriot programmes whose accreditation has lapsed. It excludes Vlerick, whose
degrees are real but open only to people who already hold a master's.

Costs are **tuition only**. Living costs in Switzerland, Ireland and the Nordics will
exceed tuition several times over; in Greece, Poland, Romania and Moldova they will
not. That inversion matters more than the tuition column and is not visible in it.
